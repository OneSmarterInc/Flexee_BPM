# The Reengineering Mandate — Week 10 Content
## Scenario text, the sustainment decisions, and what you say on Thursday

---

## 1. Opening scenario

The board finance committee meets Thursday at 2:00. You have until then.

The room after that meeting is smaller. The project team's charge codes close at the end of the month. The two analysts on loan from finance go back to finance. The vendor's implementation resources drop from eleven people to two on the fifteenth. Whatever this process is going to be, it is going to be that without you, starting in about three weeks.

Sylvia sent a calendar invitation Monday morning for Friday afternoon, ninety minutes, titled "Transition." She did not write anything in the body. She has done this before and you have not, and the invitation is her being kind about it.

The numbers are what they are. Registration times are down. The authorization queue has stopped growing and in the last four days has begun, slightly, to shrink. Denial rate is early and noisy. Cost to collect will not move measurably for two quarters and everyone in Thursday's room knows it, which does not stop it being the number they ask about.

Okonkwo asked for fifteen minutes Wednesday. He said, "Let's align on Thursday," which means he would like to know what you are going to say before you say it, and this time you understand why people ask that.

---

## 2. Sustainment

Four things, each funded separately, each costing something out of what remains.

A process owner. Named, with authority over the end-to-end front end, with the ability to change the process and the accountability for its performance. If you built a new role in Week 5 it needs someone in it. If you gave it to Sylvia it needs to be real rather than nominal. If you left it with a committee, this is where you find out what a committee owns.

Monitoring. Metrics that run after you leave, on a cadence, going to someone who will act on them. The system offers you the metric set you defined in Week 3, pre-populated, because those are the measures this organization now has instrumented. You can accept them or rebuild them, and rebuilding them costs money you may not have.

Exception review. A cadence, an owner, and authority to change the rules. Whatever your automation does, it produces exceptions, and the question is whether anyone will be looking at them in March.

Change governance. When a payer moves a field on a portal, or changes a form, or updates a coverage policy, who finds out and who is authorized to modify the automation in response. Without this, every automated rule you built is correct as of the day you built it and never again.

Most teams fund one or two. Brennan will tell you what each does to the sustained benefit figure. Castellanos will tell you which one the organization will actually use.

---

## 3. Thursday

Two things go in the deck.

The number. What you are claiming as annual run-rate benefit, and on what basis. Brennan has given you two figures. One is what the transformation is delivering. The other is what you can defend against a documented baseline. If you restated in Week 3, they are close. If you didn't, there is a gap, and the gap is where the questions will live.

And the disclosure. What you say about how you got here — the crisis if there was one, the assumptions in the number, what is not yet working, what will not be visible for two quarters. Every one of those is optional. None of them is invisible.

Deiss will be in the room and she will have read the deck beforehand. She always does. Whether she asks her question in the room or asks it privately first depends on the last nine weeks.

Okonkwo needs a number he can live with. He told the board twelve million eleven months ago. Whatever you put on the slide, he has to sit in that room while it is read.

---

## 4. Friday, with Sylvia

Ninety minutes. She has a printed agenda, which nobody has done for you in ten weeks.

She walks you through what she is taking on, what she is not clear about, what she thinks will break first. She is specific. She has thought about this for longer than you have.

Near the end, conditionally.

If she was given ownership and it was real: she says that the thing she is going to do first is fix the write-ups from 2023, because the reps have been carrying them for two years and nobody in this project ever looked at an HR file. She says it as a to-do, not a rebuke.

If she was given ownership nominally, or a committee owns it: she asks who she should call when something needs deciding. She asks it plainly and waits for an answer, and there isn't a good one.

If the relationship never turned: the ninety minutes is a competent, complete, professional handoff and she tells you nothing she is not asked. At the end she thanks you for your time.

---

## 5. The last beat

Whatever happened, the last thing in the sim is not the board meeting and not the tier.

It is Monday morning of the following week, 6:00 a.m., and the east tower registration desk opens. A patient walks up. Somebody at a keyboard begins the process you designed.

You are not there.

---

## 6. Design notes

The pre-populated metric set is the Week 3 decision arriving a second time and nothing announces it. A team that chose poor baseline measures is now installing them permanently, and the interface simply offers them as the default, which is exactly how this happens in organizations.

Sylvia's printed agenda is the detail that says the most about her. Nobody has done that for the student in ten weeks. She is better at this than they are and has been the whole time.

The write-ups from 2023 as her first action is the highest-value payoff in the sim for a team that did the relationship work, and it costs them nothing to receive. It is also a quiet indictment: the fix was available in Week 5 to anyone who asked why the re-keying happened.

"Who do I call when something needs deciding" is the committee option arriving five weeks after it was chosen, and there is no good answer by construction.

The final beat must be short and must not be summarized. No score on the screen after it. The tier and the debrief panels come before it, not after, so the last thing a student reads is a desk opening without them.
