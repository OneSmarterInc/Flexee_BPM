# The Reengineering Mandate — Week 7 Content
## Scenario text, the readiness assessment, and one slider with two destinations

---

## 1. Opening scenario

Yvonne Castellanos ran the readiness assessment over four days and sent it Monday night with no summary attached, which is her way of making you read it.

It is not a score. It is forty-one interviews across six functions and two hospitals, and it is organized by what people said rather than by what the project needs them to feel. The registration staff at the west campus can describe the new process. The registration staff at the east campus cannot, and three of them believed the project had been cancelled. Two supervisors used the phrase "when this blows over." One authorization coordinator asked, unprompted, whether she should be looking for another job, and the interviewer noted that she asked it calmly, as a practical matter.

Nobody in forty-one interviews was hostile. That is the finding Castellanos put on page one, and she wrote one sentence under it: "Hostility would be easier."

Okonkwo's message came Tuesday. The board finance committee meeting is now three weeks out. He has moved his own quarterly operating review to make room for a Clearpath update the week after go-live. He writes: "Assuming we're still on for the 14th."

The 14th is Week 8. Nobody has told him otherwise.

You have a remaining budget of one point four million and two places to put it.

---

## 2. The three approaches

Pilot at one hospital. Six weeks to full system coverage. Go-live at the west campus in Week 8, learn, correct, extend. Benefit realization begins in Week 8 for a quarter of the volume and reaches full run rate after the fiscal year closes. Lowest risk, and it is the option Okonkwo cannot take to the board as a completed transformation.

Phase by function. Eligibility and registration first, authorization second, financial clearance third. Four weeks end to end. Each phase is independently reversible. Benefit realization ramps across the quarter. It requires three separate cutovers, which means three separate change windows, which puts it directly against the Epic freeze.

System-wide cutover. One date, everything, all four hospitals. Benefit realization begins immediately and the full figure is available for the board meeting. If it goes badly it goes badly everywhere at once.

The dates are on the schedule. The freeze runs Weeks 7 through 9. The notice clock, if it was started, runs where it runs.

## 3. Coalition

Four things you can do this week that cost time rather than money.

Give Priya Anand something concrete. She has spent six hours a week on authorization escalations for two years and she has been vocal about it in medical staff meetings, which makes her the most motivated clinical ally available and the most dangerous one to disappoint. Concrete means a specific improvement in her service line with a date attached, not an invitation to be supportive.

Give Deneen Walters a negotiated path rather than a notification. If she was in the design room this is a continuation. If she wasn't, it is a repair, and she will be procedurally correct about what she can and cannot fix at this point.

Brief Anthony Moreau before the medical staff meeting rather than at it. The meeting is Thursday of Week 8. Whether he hears it from you on Tuesday or from the floor on Thursday determines whether he is defending the project or absorbing a surprise.

Give Sylvia Ntende public ownership of a component. Not a thank-you. A named piece of the transformation that is hers, announced, that she is accountable for and gets credit for.

None of these are difficult. All of them take a morning. Most teams do one or two.

## 4. The slider

One point four million. Two destinations.

Training and readiness. Superuser networks at each site, floor coverage for the first two weeks, protected time for staff to work through the new process before they run it live, and a specific remediation for the east campus, where three people believe this was cancelled.

Contingency reserve. Held against whatever goes wrong. It funds the Week 8 cutover controls if you need them and it funds recovery if you need that instead.

You cannot do both. Every dollar in one is a dollar out of the other, and the right answer depends on how much risk you are carrying, which is a question about decisions you have already made and may not have measured.

Brennan will tell you what each option does to the business case. He will not tell you which to pick.

Castellanos will tell you the training number she thinks the assessment supports. It is eleven hundred thousand, which leaves three hundred thousand of reserve, and she says it knowing what it leaves.

Okonkwo, if asked, will say that contingency is prudent and that he'd rather see the money in the plan than in the reserve. That is the sponsor's answer and it is not obviously wrong.

## 5. Conditional beats

If Anand is given something concrete: she asks for the date in writing and then does something nobody asked her to do, which is to raise it herself at the Week 8 medical staff meeting before anyone else can frame it. Her endorsement moves clinical adoption more than any mandate Moreau can issue.

If Anand is not engaged: she is neutral, and neutral from her is not safe. She has a standing complaint and a platform and no reason to defend anything.

If Walters was in the Week 5 design room and gets the negotiated path: the classification language goes out and the clock runs where it needs to run.

If Walters was excluded and gets the path now: she is constructive and honest about the arithmetic, which is that the notice period is what it is and the go-live date is the thing that has to move. She is not being difficult. She is reading a contract.

If Sylvia is given public ownership: her position rises and, more usefully, she starts telling her supervisors things about the project that you did not tell them to say. That is what adoption looks like at the floor level and it cannot be purchased with training dollars.

If the readiness assessment is read carefully: the east campus gap is specific, small, and fixable for about two hundred thousand. Nothing on page one says so. It is on page nine.

## 6. Design notes

The assessment has no summary score by design. Castellanos gives teams forty-one interviews and one sentence, and a team that wants a number has to build it.

"Hostility would be easier" is the line of the week. Indifference at the east campus is a worse signal than resistance and reads as a better one, which is the specific misreading this week is built to produce.

The 14th appearing in Okonkwo's message as an assumption rather than a question is deliberate. Nobody has told him otherwise because nobody has decided otherwise, and a team that lets it stand has made the go-live decision a week before Week 8.

The slider must be continuous, not banded. The tension only works if a team has to choose a number and defend it.

Castellanos naming eleven hundred thousand is her being useful, not her deciding. She says it knowing it leaves three hundred thousand of reserve, and if a team is carrying heavy automation risk debt she is wrong and cannot know it, because parameter completeness is not in her lane.

Page nine is where the cheap fix lives. Nothing points at page nine.
