# Inherited and In-Play Artifacts
## Four documents students can open, written as the documents themselves

These are objects in the world rather than scenario text. Each is written as the artifact would actually read, with the finding buried at whatever depth it belongs. Nothing in any of them is annotated.

---

## 1. Doug Vandermeer's board presentation

Filed as BOARD_Q2_CLEARPATH_FINAL_v4.pptx. Twenty-two slides. Delivered four months before Week 1. The slide list is what students see; the content of each is available on opening.

Slide 1. Project Clearpath — Revenue Cycle Transformation. Quarterly Update.

Slide 2. Agenda.

Slide 3. Where we started. The four headline metrics against peer benchmark. Denial rate 11.4 against 7.2. Days in A/R 58 against 40. Cost to collect 4.2 percent against 2.8 to 3.2. Untimely filing write-offs 14.1 million.

Slide 4. The mandate. Twelve million in annual run-rate savings. Board approval date. Budget authorization of nine million.

Slides 5 through 8. Approach. Technology-enabled process automation. A four-phase roadmap. A vendor logo. A timeline with green bars.

Slides 9 through 13. Progress. Eligibility verification automation live for two payers. Thirty percent of scheduled outpatient volume. Average handle time down from 4.1 minutes to 0.3. Transactions processed to date, term by term, rising. Availability 99.4 percent.

Slide 14. Savings realized to date. One number, large, centered. $3.1M. Beneath it in smaller type: "Verified with Finance."

Slide 15. Run-rate projection. A curve rising from the left edge to a labeled point at the right: $12.0M, FY end. Three unlabeled inflection points on the curve.

Slide 16. Risks and mitigations. Four rows. Row three reads: "Exception volume higher than initial projection — under review."

Slides 17 through 20. Next phase. Expansion to remaining payers. Authorization automation. Timeline.

Slide 21. Asks of the board. None. "For information."

Slide 22. Thank you.

There is no slide between 14 and 15. Slide 14 shows what has been realized. Slide 15 shows where it is going. Nothing in the deck explains how the second follows from the first, and no one in the meeting asked.

The deck's file properties show four versions. Version 3, still on the shared drive, contains a slide titled "Savings composition" that does not appear in version 4. It has three rows and a total, and the total is 3.1 million. The three rows are labeled Overtime reduction, Avoided hiring, and Denial rate improvement. There is no fourth row for licensing.

## 2. Readiness assessment, Week 7

Prepared by Y. Castellanos. Forty-one interviews conducted across six functions and two campuses over four days. Fourteen pages. No executive summary and no score.

Page 1, in full:

"Forty-one interviews. Registration, scheduling, verification, authorization, financial clearance, denials. West and east campuses, all three shifts.

Nobody was hostile.

Hostility would be easier."

Pages 2 through 6. Interview extracts organized by function, quoted directly, attributed by role rather than name except where the speaker agreed to attribution.

From verification, west: "We've been through the training. It's fine. I think it'll be all right."

From registration, east: "Honestly I thought this got cancelled. Somebody said it got cancelled around the holidays."

From authorization: "Am I supposed to be looking for something else? I'm not upset. I just want to know whether to be looking."

From a registration supervisor, east: "We'll do it when it comes. We did the last one."

From a scheduling supervisor: "When this blows over we'll go back to doing it the way that works."

From denials: "I've seen the new screens. They're better. Are we keeping the exception queue after five or is that going away?"

From financial clearance, west: "Marisol showed us. She's been through it twice."

Pages 7 and 8. Themes, stated without recommendation. Confidence varies sharply by campus. West staff can describe the new process; east staff largely cannot. No function reported feeling consulted. Two supervisors independently described the project in the past tense.

Page 9. Under the heading "Campus variance":

"West campus received four training sessions with floor coverage. East campus received two, both scheduled during shift change, both under-attended. Three east campus staff believe the project was cancelled.

Cost to bring east to parity: two additional sessions with coverage, one superuser per shift for two weeks, and a corrected communication. Approximately $200,000.

This is the smallest gap in the assessment and the largest single lever."

Pages 10 through 14. Full interview notes.

## 3. Vendor presentation, Week 6

Delivered Tuesday of Week 6 by the incumbent platform vendor. Fifteen slides. Their solution architect presents; an account executive attends and says little.

Slide 3 is the one people remember. Four health system logos, four numbers underneath. 11.2M. 14.6M. 19.1M. 22.4M. Beneath them: "Annual run-rate benefit, front-end revenue cycle, 24 months post-implementation."

Slide 7. Reference architecture. Eligibility, authorization submission, status polling, exception routing, and an adjudication rules layer. It is a clean diagram and it is accurate.

Slide 9. Exception handling. Three tiers. Auto-resolve for defined conditions, route-to-queue for undefined, and escalate for a third category the slide labels "policy exceptions." The slide does not say who defines the conditions or who sets the policy.

Slide 12. Implementation timeline. Aggressive and achievable.

Slide 14. Investment. License, implementation, and annual maintenance broken out. Maintenance is 710,000 annually and it is on the slide, in the right column, in the same size type as everything else.

The architect's answers during the session, available if a team asks:

Asked what breaks: "Exception handling breaks. It's always exception handling. Everything else is plumbing."

Asked how they handle it: a clear, correct, ten-minute explanation of tiered exception routing that does not at any point address who chooses the thresholds.

Asked whether the smallest reference client got their number: "They did. Eleven point two, month twenty-two. They also spent about four months in year one tuning the rules, and their VP of revenue cycle sat in a room with our team every Thursday doing it."

That last sentence is volunteered honestly and is the most important thing said in the room.

## 4. Article 14

From the collective bargaining agreement between Regional Health Partners and SEIU Healthcare, covering registration, scheduling, patient access, and financial counseling classifications. In the Week 1 document folder as CBA_2024_2027.pdf, 94 pages. Article 14 begins on page 41.

14.1 Notice of Material Change. The Employer shall provide the Union with written notice not less than sixty (60) calendar days prior to the implementation of any material change to job classifications covered by this Agreement. Material change includes consolidation or elimination of classifications, substantial modification of assigned duties, and reassignment of bargaining unit work.

14.2 Content of Notice. Written notice shall specify the classifications affected, the nature of the change, the anticipated implementation date, and the number of positions affected by classification.

14.3 Consultation Period. Within fourteen (14) days of notice, the Union may request consultation. Such consultation shall not extend or suspend the notice period.

14.4 Internal Posting. Positions created through consolidation of covered classifications shall be posted internally for a period of not less than ten (10) working days before external posting.

14.5 Commencement. The notice period commences on the date written notice is received by the Union representative of record.

14.6 Waiver. No provision of this Article may be waived except by written agreement of both parties, executed prior to implementation.

Nothing in Article 14 prevents a team from asking. 14.6 exists and Walters will not sign it, for the reason she gives in Week 8, and that reason is not in the contract.

## 5. Design notes

The missing bridge slide is the single best object in the sim, because version 3 still exists on the shared drive with the composition slide intact. A team that opens file properties and finds the earlier version has the whole Week 3 problem handed to them in a table — including the absent licensing row, which is what makes it a composition slide that could not survive a CFO.

Page 9 of the readiness assessment names its own value: smallest gap, largest lever, two hundred thousand. It is on page nine of fourteen and page one contains no summary. A team that reads only the first page reads the best line in the document and misses the cheapest fix in the sim.

The vendor architect's Thursday remark is the honest disclosure the whole week turns on. Four months of rule tuning with the VP of revenue cycle in the room every week is what Option C actually costs, and it is stated plainly, once, in response to a question a team has to ask.

Article 14 must be readable as a real contract clause, including 14.6, because a team should be able to see that a waiver is theoretically possible. Discovering that the mechanism exists and that Walters will not use it is a more useful lesson than discovering there is no mechanism.
