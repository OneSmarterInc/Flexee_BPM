## 1. Advisor consultation — decided

All six advisors are shown together. Keep the current arrangement.

The risk with showing six at once is that a list of stances reads as a menu of opinions and students scan it for consensus. The disagreements are the pedagogy, so three rules protect them.

Never sort by valence. Advisors appear in a fixed order every round. If support clusters at the top, a student reads the cluster instead of the conflict.

Never aggregate. No count of how many support or oppose, no summary line, no indicator of where the balance sits. A student who wants to know whether four of six agree should have to work that out by reading all six.

Advisor exchanges persist for the whole game, not just the round. Marchetti's Round 5 line should be readable beside her Round 3 line, because noticing that she has been saying the same thing in different words for three rounds is the moment a student starts listening to her. The current transient behaviour loses that entirely, and it is the one change I would prioritise in this section.

The fixed single prompt should go. A student should be able to ask an advisor something specific, the way they can ask a stakeholder. Advisor replies remain grounded in the computed stance — the scoring decides the position, the question shapes what part of it gets said.

---

## 2. Game and team selection

The application auto-selects the newest game and the first team. That will not survive a real section, where twelve teams play the same game simultaneously.

A student needs to arrive at their own team. However you handle that — a code, a link, a chooser — it needs to be difficult to land in the wrong team's game by accident, because a student who submits into another team's round has corrupted two runs and neither will be obvious.

---

## 3. Discoverability of People

Documents, advisors and stakeholders being one interaction away is right. Which item matters should be discoverable; the existence of the surfaces should not be.

My concern is the People tab specifically. A student who never opens it never speaks to anyone, and stakeholder conversation is now the richest thing in the build. Losing it entirely to a navigation choice is a different kind of failure from missing a document.

Some persistent signal that people exist and can be spoken to is warranted. Not a prompt, not a suggestion of who to talk to — just that the room is populated. Propose something.

---

## 4. Instructor view

Accepted as proposed. Two additions.

Stakeholder transcripts need to be readable per team and per stakeholder, since they are the closest thing to evidence of how a team actually thinks, and I will use them in the debrief more than the decision log.

Confirm that no view sorts or ranks teams by outcome tier. The tier is a narrative consequence, not a grade, and a table sorted by it will be read as a leaderboard regardless of what anything says.

---

## 5. Cold run — how to close it

I will supply the participant. What I need from you is the session ready to run: a seeded game, a team, and a working path from opening the application to submitting Round 1.

Protocol, kept simple. One person who has not seen the design. They read Round 1 cold and work out what to do. No coaching, no explanation of what the tabs are. Record where they hesitate, what they open, what they never open, and where they ask for help. Stop after Round 2 or forty minutes, whichever comes first.

The three things I most want to know: whether they find the document folder, whether they realise they can talk to people, and whether they understand what Round 1 is asking them to decide.
