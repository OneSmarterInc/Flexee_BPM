# Phase 5
## Interface, instructor view, and pilot readiness

Phase 4 is closed. Panel three now resolves on exact access events, and Path A returning seven of ten doors closed is the right result — that inventory will start an argument in a classroom, which is what it is for. 250 tests.

Everything from here is about a room full of graduate students in the fall. The engine is correct. What is untested is whether the thing is usable by people who did not build it.

---

## 1. The interface proposal

You owe me a proposal rather than a finished interface. Show me something and I will react to it.

Six requirements from the phase 4 instructions remain load-bearing and are not open to redesign: the Round 6 threshold field with no default, the Round 8 rollback control showing the team's own words back, the four-indicator readiness dashboard with no composite score, the Round 10 metrics pre-populated from Round 3 without indication, the paginated readiness assessment, and the sixty-three item document folder with version 3 reachable through file properties.

Everything else is yours. What I want to see in the proposal.

How a round reads. Scenario text, levers, advisors, stakeholders, documents — what is on screen at once, what requires navigation, and what a student sees first when a round opens.

How advisor consultation is entered. The stances are computed and rendered. Whether a student requests one advisor at a time, sees all six, or something else, is a design decision with real consequences: showing all six at once flattens the disagreements into a list, and the disagreements are the pedagogy.

How stakeholder conversation is surfaced alongside everything else. It is now genuine conversation and it competes for attention with the levers. A student who never notices they can talk to people has lost most of the simulation.

How the document folder behaves. Sixty-three items is a lot on a screen and the disorder is deliberate.

---

## 2. The instructor view

This has existed as a projection since phase 1 and has never been designed for a person.

What it needs to do, in order of importance. Show me, before class, which teams reached which outcome and what their panel three looks like, because that is what I will run the debrief from. Let me open any team's decision log and read what they actually chose. Let me read stakeholder transcripts, which are the richest thing in the system and currently invisible to me.

Two things it must not do. It must not surface exposure values or designed truths in a way that could reach a student screen. And it must not compute a class ranking — the outcome tier is not a grade and the interface should not imply otherwise.

---

## 3. Pilot readiness

Three things I need before a real section plays this.

A run-through by someone who has not seen the design. Not a test. A person who reads the Round 1 text cold and tries to work out what to do. I want to know where they get stuck, what they never notice, and whether they find the document folder at all. One person is enough to surface most of it.

A reset and seed path, so I can create a section's worth of teams quickly and start a game over when a class goes sideways.

A statement of what happens when things break mid-round. A student loses connection, a team submits twice, a round is submitted with a required field empty. These are ordinary classroom events and I would rather know the behaviour than discover it in week three.

---

## 4. What closes this phase

The interface proposal, the instructor view, and the cold run-through report.

The cold run-through is the one I care most about. Everything else in this build has been verified against a specification. That is the first test against a person.
